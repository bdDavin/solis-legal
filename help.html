How Solis works

Last updated: August 2026

Solis shows the sun's path through the sky for any place on Earth, on any day of the year. Move the map to pick a place, tap the date pill to change the day, or tap AR to see the path overhead.

What's free, what's paid

Solis is free to use. The map, the dial, search and the AR view all work without paying anything, for any address, for today.

Choosing a date other than today is the paid feature. That's what unlocks seeing what the light does in July while you're standing somewhere in November. You can buy it once (Lifetime) or monthly — both prices are shown in the app before anything is charged, and the Lifetime unlock is a one-time purchase that does not renew.

Features

The dial

The coloured ring on the map shows the sun's path through the sky at the crosshair. Hour dots run sunrise › noon › sunset, warming from horizon red through golden orange to high-noon gold. Tilt the map with two fingers and the dial lifts into a 3D sky-dome.

Solis has no obstruction model, so the path is what the sky does at that spot, not what a neighbouring roof or tree leaves of it. The AR view is there so you can see those for yourself.

Pick a place

Drag, pinch and rotate the map to inspect any location on Earth. Tap the search icon to jump to an address, a landmark, or one of your recent picks.

Pick a date

Tap the date pill to change the day. The year doesn't matter — the sun's path is the same on the same day every year — so you can compare summers or seek out winter twilight. Tap Today in the picker to snap back to right now.

AR view

Tap the AR tab to overlay the sun's path on the real sky around you. AR uses your camera and compass; point your phone north and the arc rises east and sets west. The yellow sphere marks where the sun is right now. Zoom in to mark the arc every 30, 15 or 5 minutes; zoom out for fewer marks.

Recenter

The location button cycles three states: off, centred on you (north-up), and follow-heading, where the map rotates with your phone. Pan the map and tracking turns off automatically. On devices without a magnetometer the button toggles between off and centred.

Tips

Polar regions

At very high latitudes the sun may never rise (polar night) or never set (midnight sun). AR shows a banner, and the dial's path falls entirely above or below the compass ring.

Local time, not yours

Times shown are the local time at the place under the crosshair, not on your phone. Look at New York from Stockholm and Solis reads in New York time.

Appearance and clock format

Settings › Appearance switches between System, Light and Dark. Settings › Clock toggles 12-hour and 24-hour labels on the dial, in AR, and on the date pill.

Troubleshooting

Compass needs calibration

If AR shows a "Calibrate compass" pill, hold your phone in front of you and wave it slowly in a figure-8 a few times. iOS recalibrates the magnetometer, and the pill disappears once the compass is accurate enough to align the arc with true north.

Location is off

Solis needs your coordinates to centre on you. If the map shows a "Location access is off" message, open iOS Settings › Privacy & Security › Location Services › Solis and grant "While Using the App". The dial works regardless: it is computed for the crosshair, not for your device.

No camera for AR

AR requires a rear camera. On hardware without one, the AR tab explains that AR isn't available on this device.

I paid and it isn't unlocked

Settings › Purchase › Restore Purchases. If that doesn't do it, email hello@trysolis.app and include your Support ID from the same screen.

Safety

Never look directly at the sun. Solis is informational guidance only; use at your own risk.
